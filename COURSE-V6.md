## Six new stages

**Keigo you can build** — the gap left by the swap table. お〜になる turns almost
any verb honorific and お〜する turns it humble, which is what makes keigo
learnable rather than memorisable. Plus 〜させていただきます, and the rule that
decides which dial: whose action, which side of the inside–outside line, how
formal the setting. Talking to a client about your own manager, you use humble
forms — rank inside your company is irrelevant.

**Particles, second pass** — だけ, しか and ばかり for *only*, where しか is
grammatically negative and the English translation is not. さえ, こそ, でも for
*even*. Stacked particles (には, では, とは). And までに against まで, which is the
difference between a deadline and a duration.

**Just now, still, about to** — 〜たばかり measures how recent it feels, 〜たところ
measures the clock, so you can say 日本に来たばかり months after arriving. Then
why まだ食べません means you are refusing rather than have not yet, and 〜ようになる
for the sentence a learner most wants to say.

**Keeping a conversation going** — fillers as turn-holding rather than sloppiness,
repairing what you missed by naming the specific word rather than asking for a
repeat, and the signposts for changing subject and leaving. そろそろ is a warning
shot, and going without it is abrupt.

**Vocabulary by place** — food, home and routine, work and school, travel and
money. Four themed sets, including 先輩 and 後輩, which mark who arrived first
rather than who is older and decide your register for years.

**Grammar for reading** — として, において, に関して, によって; the whole わけ family,
where わけではない denies an inference without asserting its opposite; and the
formal registers of must and cannot, including お答えしかねます, a refusal so
polite it barely looks like one.

## Three new lessons in existing stages

Dialect awareness — that 標準語 is what you are learning and Kansai-ben is what
you will hear, with the systematic swaps (や, へん, あかん, ほんま) so an
unfindable word does not cost you an afternoon. Era names and the relative-time
words that reach further than English does in one word (おととい, 再来年, 三年ぶり).
And practising output, which nothing on this site otherwise builds — shadowing,
three sentences a day, and parsing what you wrote.
