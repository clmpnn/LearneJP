## Six new stages

**Counting things properly** — the sound changes nobody warns you about. 一本 is
いっぽん, 三本 is さんぼん, 六本 is ろっぽん, and it is regular: h-initial counters
go to p after 1, 6, 8, 10 and to b after 3. Plus the ten counters worth having,
ordinals, fractions running backwards from English, and 三時 versus 三時間.

**Seems, looks like, apparently** — the classic five-way confusion. そう does two
unrelated jobs depending on what it attaches to: おいしそうです is *looks tasty*
from your own eyes, おいしいそうです is *I hear it is tasty*. Then よう, みたい,
らしい (which also means typical-of) and っぽい, sorted by evidence and register.
Closes with why Japanese hedges so much, and why 道を聞きたいんですが never
finishes its sentence.

**Making words from words** — the negating prefixes 不, 無, 非, 未; the suffixes
that change word class (的, 化, 性, 者, 家, 員, 屋, さ, み); and compound verbs,
where 読み始める is two verbs you already know and only the second one conjugates.

**Coming and going** — 行く and 来る work from the speaker's position, not the
listener's, so you 行きます to a friend's party where English says *come*. Then
〜ていく and 〜てくる carrying direction through time, and the を that marks a
route rather than an object.

**Writing to people** — です・ます体 versus だ・である体 and holding one per
document; the email frame, where opening with your actual request reads as
abrupt; and how to read the signals that the register has shifted.

**What the language assumes** — the why behind patterns already learned. Refusal
without the word no (ちょっと and 難しいですね are both complete refusals).
Apology as social lubricant, and how it shows up as grammar in the suffering
passive and the causative-request. And what gets omitted, and why repeating 私は
faintly insists on yourself.

## Three new lessons in existing stages

Kanji that differ by one stroke — 待/持, 未/末, 大/犬 — and why components beat
silhouettes. ために versus ように and ことにする versus ことになる, which is how
you announce a transfer without sounding boastful. And how to find material you
can actually read.

## Where I would stop

The course now runs from the first kana into solid N4 and touches N3. Four rounds
in, the remaining gaps are narrow — more counters, more set phrases, more
patterns — and each is worth less than the last. If you want the page to keep
getting better, the higher-value work is now in the tools rather than the text:
marking a lesson as read the way steps are ticked, letting the quizzes draw from
a stage you are on, or having the Sentence tab link back to the lesson that
explains the pattern it just found.
